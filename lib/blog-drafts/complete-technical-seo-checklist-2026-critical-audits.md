    <h2>2. Crawlability and Indexation Checklist: 10 Points That Determine If Google Can Find You</h2>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">The 10 foundational crawlability and indexation checkpoints are: (1) Robots.txt: confirm no important pages, CSS, or scripts are blocked; (2) XML Sitemap: submitted to Search Console and updated within 24 hours of new content; (3) Crawl Budget: verify Googlebot spends crawl allowance on priority pages, not pagination or parameterized URLs; (4) Redirect Chains: ensure every 301 resolves in a single hop with zero chains; (5) Canonical Tags: verify every page has a self-referencing canonical, and duplicate variants point to the main source.</p>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Continuing the checklist: (6) Index Coverage: check Google Search Console for zero unhandled 'Excluded by noindex' notices on target pages; (7) Internal Linking Depth: keep all high-value pages reachable within 3 clicks from the homepage; (8) URL Structure: keep URLs clean, lowercase, and hyphen-separated without underscores; (9) Pagination: implement rel=next/prev annotations or canonical consolidation; (10) Hreflang: implement reciprocal tags if you serve multiple regions or languages.</p>

    <h2>3. Core Web Vitals Checklist: INP, LCP and CLS Thresholds Every Page Must Meet</h2>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Core Web Vitals are confirmed Google ranking signals with concrete thresholds. The three metrics every site must meet in 2026 are: Interaction to Next Paint (INP) below 200ms; Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) below 2.5 seconds; Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) under 0.1. Pages that miss these numbers give up rank to faster competitors. In our audits, main-thread blocking JavaScript is the most common cause of poor INP. Unoptimized hero banners hurt LCP most often, while missing width and height attributes on images trigger unexpected layout shifts.</p>

    <h2>4. Structured Data and Schema Markup Checklist for Rich Results and AI Overviews</h2>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Structured data acts as the technical bridge between your page content and search engines, powering both traditional rich results and generative AI citations. The structured data checklist includes: (1) Article schema with author, datePublished, and dateModified on blog posts; (2) FAQPage schema on pages with question-based sections; (3) HowTo schema on step-by-step guides; (4) Product and AggregateRating schema on ecommerce catalog items; (5) LocalBusiness schema on service pages with verified NAP details; (6) BreadcrumbList schema for navigation breadcrumbs; (7) SiteLinksSearchBox schema on the homepage. Always validate schemas with Google's Rich Results Test.</p>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">To optimize for modern AI search engines, add: (8) Organization schema with sameAs links on the homepage; (9) Person schema with verified credentials on About and author pages; (10) SpeakableSpecification schema on content targeting voice assistants. These schemas give search bots unambiguous machine-readable context about your business entity.</p>

    <h2>5. Mobile-First Indexing and Page Experience Checklist: What Google Measures in 2026</h2>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Google evaluates the mobile version of your page as the primary canonical version under mobile-first indexing, even when most of your visitors use desktop screens. The mobile checklist requires: (1) Responsive layouts without horizontal scrolling on 375px viewports; (2) Touch targets with at least 48x48 pixel tap areas; (3) Font sizes of 16px or larger for readable body copy; (4) No outdated or unsupported mobile scripts; (5) Identical content between mobile and desktop layouts so mobile visitors see the exact same information.</p>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Broader page experience signals also count. Your site must serve all pages over HTTPS with an active SSL certificate. Intrusive full-screen popups on mobile trigger direct ranking penalties. Review your security standing in Google Search Console to verify zero malware flags. Check your HTTPS redirects and security headers using our <a href='/tools/http-header-checker' style='color:#2563eb;font-weight:700'>HTTP & SSL Header Checker</a>.</p>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Audit mobile usability warnings directly in Google Search Console under Mobile Usability, and resolve any viewport or tap target errors before rolling out major content campaigns.</p>
    

    <h2>6. Security, International and Advanced Technical SEO Checklist</h2>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Advanced technical SEO includes several areas that teams easily overlook: (1) Log File Analysis: inspect server logs to verify Googlebot spends its time on priority pages; (2) JavaScript Rendering: ensure all vital text and links render in the initial server HTML, not only via client-side hydration; (3) International SEO: configure hreflang tags with reciprocal links when serving multiple countries; (4) Faceted Navigation: apply canonical tags or robots rules to stop parameter bloat from product filters; (5) Security Headers: deploy Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, and HSTS headers for added trust and security.</p>
    <p style="line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:14px;">Security issues directly impact search visibility. Sites flagged for malware or deceptive downloads in Google's Safe Browsing database get warning interstitials in Chrome and rapid ranking drops. Review Google Search Console's Security Issues report monthly, and keep third-party scripts closely audited.</p>
