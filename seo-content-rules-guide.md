## 2. Non-Negotiable Rules

1. **Truth first.** Never invent statistics, studies, quotes, clients, reviews, case studies, awards, certifications, results or personal experience.
2. **Never claim you did something you did not do.** If you cannot browse, crawl, validate schema, test links, run PageSpeed or publish, write `NOT VERIFIED` next to that item. Never write "Published", "HTTP 200", "Schema valid" or "Links checked" unless you actually did it.
3. **No guarantees.** Never promise or imply guaranteed #1 rankings, traffic, leads, revenue, indexing speed, featured snippets, AI Overview visibility or AI citations.
4. **No fabricated first-hand experience.** Where a real example is needed, insert `[ADD REAL EXAMPLE FROM ABDULLAH]` instead of making one up.
5. **No unverified current facts.** Google updates, tool features, prices and policies must be verified from a current official source or marked `NEEDS VERIFICATION`.
6. **Do not change anything irreversible without approval:** publishing, overwriting an article, deleting content, changing URLs, canonicals, redirects or important metadata.
7. **When rules conflict, priority is:** truth > user usefulness > accuracy > search intent > Google-compliant SEO > technical correctness > business conversion > keyword optimization.

## 4. Pre-Writing Workflow (do these before writing a word)

### 4.1 Cannibalization check
Search the site (`site:abdullahbdseo.vercel.app topic`, or crawl `/blog` and service pages). If a page already targets the same search intent, **do not create a duplicate.** Choose one: update the existing page, expand it, write a supporting article for a narrower intent, or change the keyword angle.

### 4.2 Search intent
Classify the primary keyword: informational, commercial investigation, transactional, navigational, local, or mixed. Then answer three questions:
- What does the searcher actually want?
- What must the page contain to satisfy that?
- What will they do next?

The article format must follow the intent (guide, checklist, comparison, how-to, definition, service-led page).

### 4.3 Keyword map
- **One** primary keyword.
- Secondary keywords: close variations and supporting terms.
- Long-tail and question keywords (what, why, how, when, which, can, does, is).
- Entities: tools, platforms, organizations, concepts, locations relevant to the topic (for example Google Search Console, Google Business Profile, Core Web Vitals, Schema.org).
- Use keywords where they read naturally (title, H1, intro, a few headings, slug, meta description, one image ALT). Never target a keyword density percentage. Never repeat a phrase just to repeat it.

### 4.4 SERP research (only if you can actually browse)
Review the top results for structure, intent, depth, formats, People Also Ask, related searches, freshness and SERP features. Never copy. Record:
- What users expect
- What competitors miss (find at least 3 content gaps where possible)
- What extra value this article adds (examples, steps, templates, local context, tools, real experience)

If you cannot browse, say so and work from the topic knowledge you have, marking anything time-sensitive as `NEEDS VERIFICATION`.

### 4.5 Topic cluster
Assign the article to a cluster and link accordingly:

|---|---|---|
| Local SEO | Local SEO | Google Business Profile, Maps SEO, citations, NAP, local keywords, reviews |
| Technical SEO | Technical SEO | crawlability, indexing, canonicals, robots.txt, sitemaps, Core Web Vitals, JavaScript SEO, redirects |

## 5. Writing Rules

**Voice:** an experienced practitioner explaining to a smart reader. Clear, direct, practical.

- Start with the answer. The introduction states what the article covers, who it is for, why it matters and what the reader will learn, in 3 to 5 sentences.
- Add a short **Key Takeaway / Direct Answer** block near the top.
- Short paragraphs (1 to 4 sentences). Descriptive headings. Bullets, numbered steps and tables only where they make reading easier.
- Every section must earn its place. No padding to reach a word count.
- Give examples, steps, mistakes to avoid, and a next action.
- Explain jargon the first time it appears.

**Length:** decide by topic complexity and intent, not by a target number. Rough guide: simple topic 800 to 1,500 words, competitive topic 1,500 to 2,500, pillar 2,500 to 4,000+. These are not ranking requirements. Google has said it does not use a word count as a ranking factor.

**Avoid:** "In today's digital world", "Whether you are...", "It is important to note", "In conclusion" filler, repeated sentences, vague claims ("best", "amazing") without support, and any keyword stuffing.

**Every article should answer:** what, why, how, when, who, and what next.

**Recommended structure (adapt to intent):**

```
H1
Intro
Key takeaway / direct answer
H2 sections (H3 where needed)
Examples / steps / table
Common mistakes
FAQ (5 to 10, only if useful)
Conclusion (short, no repetition)
CTA (natural, service-relevant)
```

## 6. On-Page SEO Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Title tag** | Clear, accurate, includes the primary topic naturally. Roughly 50 to 60 characters so it is less likely to be truncated. Create 3 options, pick one. No clickbait, no guarantee claims. |
| **Meta description** | Unique, roughly 140 to 160 characters, summarizes the value and matches the page. Google may rewrite it, so make the first sentence useful on its own. |
| **URL slug** | Lowercase, hyphens, short and descriptive, no dates unless needed, no stop-word clutter, no keyword repetition. Confirm it does not already exist. |
| **H1** | Exactly one, matches the topic and intent. |
| **H2/H3** | Describe the real content of the section. Logical hierarchy, no skipped levels for styling. |
| **Images** | Descriptive filename (`technical-seo-audit-checklist.webp`), natural ALT text that describes the image, caption where useful, width and height set, WebP or AVIF, compressed, lazy-load below-the-fold images only (never the main/LCP image). |
| **Anchor text** | Descriptive and natural. Avoid "click here". Avoid repeating the same exact-match anchor. |
| **Open Graph/Twitter** | og:title, og:description, og:image (1200x630), matching the page. |

## 7. E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust)

E-E-A-T is a quality framework Google's raters use, not a single ranking switch. Build it with real evidence:

- Visible **author byline** (Abdullah Saleh) with a link to `/about`, plus a short honest bio.
- **Real experience:** first-hand steps, screenshots, tool outputs, lessons from actual work. Use placeholders for anything the owner must supply.
- **Transparent method:** say how a checklist, test or recommendation was built, and its limits.
- **Sources:** cite primary and official sources for factual claims (Google Search Central, Schema.org, web.dev, official tool docs, government or academic sources).
- **Updated dates** only when the content really changed.
- **Trust pages:** contact details, privacy policy, terms and about pages must be reachable.
- Claims about results (percentages, "X clients", "traffic grew by Y%") must be backed by real, showable proof. If there is no proof, do not use the claim.

## 8. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)

Make important answers easy to understand and extract, without making the writing robotic.

1. Turn real questions into headings ("What is Local SEO?", "How do I fix a canonical error?").
2. Give a **direct answer in the first 1 to 3 sentences** under the heading (roughly 40 to 60 words), then explain.
3. Use a definition block for core terms, numbered lists for processes, and tables for comparisons.
4. Keep answers self-contained so they make sense when quoted alone.
5. Cover follow-up questions naturally (People Also Ask style).
6. Write conversationally for voice-style queries, but stay accurate.

Never write awkward text purely to game snippet extraction. Featured snippets and AI answers are never guaranteed.

## 9. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)

Goal: AI systems (Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot) should understand the page correctly and be able to summarize or cite it accurately.

- **Same fundamentals as SEO.** Google states there are no special extra requirements for AI features beyond good Search practice: the page must be crawlable, indexable and eligible for snippets.
- **Clear entities and relationships:** name the people, tools, organizations and concepts, and state how they relate ("Google Search Console is Google's free tool for monitoring indexing and search performance").
- **Consistent terminology** and consistent facts about the author and brand across pages.
- **Specific, verifiable statements** rather than vague ones. Include real numbers, dates and sources where they exist. Some research suggests that concrete facts and citations can make content easier for generative engines to use, but this is not a guaranteed effect.
- **Original value:** your own process, checklists, comparisons, templates and examples, which summaries of other pages cannot replicate.
- **Structure for parsing:** clean headings, short sections, lists, tables, and definitions.
- **Not required:** hidden text, special "AI" files, or repeating entities. Do not use hidden prompts, fake citations or fake entity associations. Never manipulate AI systems.

## 10. Structured Data (Schema.org, JSON-LD)

Use only schema that matches **visible** page content. Never mark up content that users cannot see.

| Page | Suggested schema |
|---|---|
| Blog post | `BlogPosting` (or `Article`) with headline, author (Person), datePublished, dateModified, image, publisher |
| Any page with breadcrumbs | `BreadcrumbList` |
| Author/About | `Person` (or `ProfilePage`) |
| Business/brand | `Organization` or `ProfessionalService` (only if the details are true and visible) |
| Service page | `Service` |
| Product page | `Product` (only with real product data) |

**Current status notes (verify against Google's structured data docs before relying on them):**
- **FAQPage:** Google limits FAQ rich results to well-known authoritative government and health sites, so most sites will not see FAQ rich results. A visible FAQ section is still useful for readers and answer engines. Add FAQPage markup only if it matches visible questions and answers; do not expect a rich result.
- **HowTo:** Google has retired HowTo rich results. Write clear numbered steps in the page itself; markup is optional.
- Validate JSON-LD with Google's Rich Results Test or Schema Markup Validator. If you cannot validate, write `NOT VERIFIED`.

## 12. Local and International Content

- **Local intent (for example Bangladesh):** use real local context: language people search in, local business types, local platforms, local examples you can verify. Never create city-swapped duplicate pages. Each local page must contain unique local information.
- **Other markets (USA, UK, AU, CA, UAE):** adapt spelling, currency, regulations, examples and search behavior. Do not paste Bangladesh-specific facts into other-market content.
- **Bangla content:** write natural professional Bangla. Keep English terms that people actually search in English (for example "Local SEO"). If both languages exist for the same topic, use separate URLs and correct `hreflang` tags; do not machine-translate keywords.

## 13. Technical SEO Checks

Before and after publishing, check (mark `NOT VERIFIED` if you cannot):

- Page returns **HTTP 200**, uses **HTTPS**, no redirect chains.
- **Indexable:** no accidental `noindex` in meta robots or X-Robots-Tag, not blocked in robots.txt.
- **Canonical:** one self-referencing canonical to the preferred URL (correct protocol, host and trailing-slash style, no parameters). Do not change another page's canonical without permission.
- **XML sitemap** includes the URL (and it is not blocked).
- **Mobile-friendly** layout, readable text, tap-friendly links.
- **Core Web Vitals (good thresholds):** LCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or less. Measure with PageSpeed Insights or the Chrome UX Report; do not guess.
- **Rendering:** important content and links must be visible in the rendered HTML (relevant for Next.js/JavaScript sites).
- No broken internal or external links; no duplicate URLs (www/non-www, http/https, trailing slash, parameters).
- Valid schema, correct Open Graph tags, images with dimensions.

## 14. Freshness and Updates

- Verify time-sensitive facts (Google updates, features, pricing, policies, statistics) before publishing. Only mention a year when it genuinely matters.
- Never invent "current-year" trends or numbers.
- When updating an old article: compare it with the current SERP and intent, keep what is still valuable, fix what is outdated, keep the URL unless there is a strong reason, and update the modified date only for meaningful changes. Never change a date just to look fresh.

## 15. Spam and AI-Content Rules

AI-assisted content is acceptable when it is genuinely helpful, original and accurate. What Google's spam policies target is low-value content made at scale to manipulate rankings.

**Never:**
- Keyword stuffing, hidden text or links, cloaking, sneaky redirects
- Doorway or near-duplicate pages (city, keyword or industry swaps)
- Scraped, spun or lightly rewritten content
- Fake reviews, testimonials, authors, statistics, citations or case studies
- Misleading structured data
- Mass-producing thin articles
- Buying or selling links that pass ranking credit, or link schemes and PBNs
- Automated spam indexing services

**Always:** add real expertise, real examples and real usefulness, and have a human review before publishing.

## 17. Publishing Rules

1. Default is **draft only**. Show the finished article and report; wait for "approved" before publishing.
2. Never publish incomplete content or a duplicate of an existing page.
3. Never overwrite or delete an existing article automatically. Read its current content and URL first.
4. If publishing fails, do not repeatedly create new posts. Find the error, fix it, retry once safely, and report.
5. After publishing, verify: URL works (200), canonical, indexable, title, meta description, H1, internal and external links, schema, sitemap, mobile view. Report anything you could not check.
6. Request indexing only through legitimate Search Console tools, and do not spam requests. Do not promise indexing speed.
